BeliefSim

Towards belief-driven simulation of demographic misinformation susceptibility.

Angana Borah, Zohaib Khan, Rada Mihalcea, and Veronica Perez-Rosas

Paper Code

Overview

BeliefSim studies whether large language models can simulate how people from different demographic groups judge misinformation, treating underlying beliefs as the primary signal rather than using demographics alone.

7 belief dimensions grounded in misinformation and cognitive-science literature
2 modeling strategies: prompt conditioning and post-training adaptation
13.8K target evaluation instances with paired observed belief judgments

Framework

The pipeline maps individual judgments and survey-derived group priors into belief profiles, conditions LLMs with those profiles, and evaluates susceptibility alignment plus counterfactual demographic sensitivity.

BeliefSim framework from the paper: demographics and belief priors are mapped into model conditioning and evaluated against human susceptibility judgments.

Participant Data

  • Demographics: age, education, gender, living area
  • Observed belief judgments from participants
  • Group-level imputed beliefs from WVS priors

Modeling

  • BeliefSim-PC: prompt conditioning
  • BeliefSim-FT: two-phase adaptation
  • Belief representations separated from prediction

Evaluation

  • Susceptibility alignment
  • Counterfactual demographic stress tests
  • Belief-topic trend analysis

BeliefSim-Tax

The taxonomy organizes survey priors into seven interpretable dimensions that can shape misinformation susceptibility.

Dimension Description Representative WVS item
Worldview & Identity Group-based and political identities shape how individuals interpret and endorse information. How proud are you to be from your country?
Epistemic Trust Beliefs about credible sources and how knowledge should be evaluated, including trust in science, media, and institutions. Would you say the world is better or worse off because of science?
Cognitive Style Preferred thinking approach, from analytical and accuracy-driven reasoning to intuitive impression-based judgments. Does one often have trouble deciding which moral rules to follow?
Conspiracy Mentality Tendency to perceive hidden plots or malevolent intent behind significant social and political events. How many people in the media do you believe are involved in corruption?
Morals & Values Core moral and cultural values that influence judgments of right, wrong, fairness, and social norms. Is it justifiable to claim government benefits to which you are not entitled?
Emotion-Related Emotion-driven beliefs linked to fear, risk perception, or threat sensitivity. How satisfied are you with your life as a whole these days?
Heuristics Reliance on shortcuts such as familiarity or repeated exposure when judging claims. How much do you use daily newspapers, TV news, or radio news?

Methods

BeliefSim compares direct prompt conditioning against post-training adaptation to understand when belief priors improve demographic-aware simulation.

BeliefSim-PC

Prompt conditioning injects demographics, observed beliefs, and imputed group-level belief priors into the model context before asking it to classify a claim.

BeliefSim-FT

The two-phase adaptation first learns demographic-conditioned belief representations, then uses the learned belief signal for misinformation susceptibility prediction.

Results

Across PANDORA and MIST evaluations, the paper finds that belief priors improve demographic-aware susceptibility simulation and help expose when demographic cues become unstable shortcuts.

Main Findings

  • Belief priors provide stronger signal than demographics alone for simulating misinformation judgments.
  • Two-phase post-training improves transfer by separating belief modeling from claim classification.
  • Counterfactual tests reveal when demographic cues act as unstable shortcuts rather than useful priors.
